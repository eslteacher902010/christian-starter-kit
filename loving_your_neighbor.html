Lesson Ten

Loving Your Neighbor

"Go and do likewise." (Luke 10:37)

The Story of the Good Samaritan

(Luke 10:33-34) "But a certain Samaritan, as he journeyed, came where he was. And when he saw him, he had compassion. So he went to him and bandaged his wounds, pouring on oil and wine; and he set him on his own animal, brought him to an inn, and took care of him."

Love of God and love of neighbor are the two great commandments. As the Good Samaritan shows us, true love perceives a need, bothers to get involved in the "mess," and pays the price to follow through to completion—all with an open-hearted attitude.

Fulfilling the Command

Step One: Immediate Help

Never ignore the need. Give what you can from your resources. To "close our hearts" is to squelch the love of God in us (1 John 3:17).

Step Two: Referral & Support

Refer them to those more able to help (the "innkeeper"), but stay involved. Stand with them until things are sorted out.

Step Three: Check Attitude

Are you being generous or resenting the interruption? Even if motives are mixed, keep helping—it keeps your heart open to God's correction.

Priorities for Compassion

While we are to "do good to all," the Bible suggests an order of priority for our care:

  1. Our Spouse
  2. Our Children
  3. Our Aged Parents
  4. The Church (Household of Faith)
  5. The basic needs of the world

A Final Note

This is a common-sense order to help you sort out your role, but never use it as an excuse! Remember, Galatians 6:10 exhorts us to "do good to all" as we have the opportunity.
