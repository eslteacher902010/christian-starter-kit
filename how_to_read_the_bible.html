Lesson Two

How To Read The Bible

"Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path." (Psalm 119:105)

One of the first questions we ask is "how can I understand the Bible?" The Bible is a book that comes alive to Christians but lacks impact to those who don't believe. In today's devotion, we will look at how to read the Bible effectively.

Where to Start?

The best place to start is with the Gospels: Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John. These books document the life of Jesus and help us to believe. You can find them at the beginning of the New Testament, about three-quarters of the way into your Bible.

Pro Tip: Try to get a Bible translation that speaks to your heart in a language you understand. You can trust major translations like the NIV or NKJV to be accurate to the original Hebrew and Greek texts.

How to Dig Deeper

When you read, take a pen and notebook. Read about a chapter at a time and ask yourself these questions:

  • What is this saying about God?
  • What is this saying about Jesus?
  • Is there a command to obey?
  • Is there a principle to learn?
  • Is there a prayer I can pray?
  • How can I apply it today?

Practical Advice

Find a quiet place free from distraction. Whether it's late at night in an armchair or early in the morning, consistency is more important than "magic." Use your mind, think hard, and allow it to speak to your heart.

As you read, pay special attention to things God repeats: commands like love one another, forgive, and do not judge. Major on these things, and avoid getting distracted by useless arguments or quarreling about words.

Study Tools to Consider:

  • • Concordances & Bible Dictionaries
  • • Commentaries (found in Christian bookstores)
  • • Computer Programs (Logos, QuickVerse, Online Bible)

(2 Timothy 2:14) "Warn them before God against quarreling about words; it is of no value, and only ruins those who listen."
